## Overview

Swing analysis today means either an expensive simulator or a coach's eye.
This second-year materials project asked whether a **sticker on the club
face** could do part of the job: a mechanochromic material that changes colour
where — and how hard — the ball strikes, visible at a glance, then resets for
the next swing. The work is a structured materials study: derive the loads,
set requirements, screen the candidate chemistry and physics, and select the
most promising route.

## From swing to specification

A driver strikes the ball with up to **9,000 N**. FEA of the impact shows the
42.70 mm ball flattening to a 23.40 mm contact patch — an area of
4.3×10⁻⁴ m², giving a design stress of **21 MPa**. Watching real practice
rhythm set the second requirement: the colour change must be readable and
then reset in roughly **10–15 seconds**, so every swing gets a fresh canvas.
The material must also survive 21 MPa without damage, add negligible mass,
and behave as much like the club face as possible.

## Screening the candidates

Three material classes went through the filter:

**Mechanophore-linked polymers** — molecular switches (spiropyran, rhodamine,
carbon-centred mechanoradicals) that change colour when a bond is stretched.
Elegant chemistry, but the activation thresholds sit at **single
nanonewtons** per molecule: rhodamine and CABBF discolour far too easily for
a driving range, and rhodamine's reset (3 minutes under UV, up to 24 hours at
room temperature) misses the 10–15 s target by orders of magnitude.

**Chemiluminescent polymers** — emit light as bonds sever. Disqualified
immediately: the effect is **not reversible**, and a one-shot sticker is not a
training tool.

**Mechanochromic photonic materials** — colour from _structure_, not
chemistry: nanoscale layers create a photonic bandgap, and pressure changes
the layer spacing `d` in Bragg's law (2d·sinθ = nλ), shifting which
wavelength reflects. Fast response, chemically stable, resistant to
photobleaching — and the colour change scales with deformation, exactly the
quantity a swing analysis needs.

## The proposed design

The selected concept is the layered sticker in the hero image: a
**mechanochromic photonic layer sandwiched between protective polymer
skins**, so the fragile optical structure never takes the hit directly. For
the carrier, FEA on an acrylate layer under the 21 MPa impact confirmed it
survives with strains in the 10⁻³ range — where a soft alternative like PDMS
(yield ≈ 700 kPa) would fail thirty-fold below the requirement.

## Honest limits

This is a **literature-based selection**, and the report says so plainly: no
candidate could be crowned without lab work. Regeneration behaviour under
varying sunlight is under-documented for the mechanophores, and the photonic
route's core challenge — manufacturing layers at exactly the thickness
Bragg's law demands — is identified but not solved. The conclusion is a
direction with a test plan, not a product.

## Lessons learned

- **Requirements turn chemistry into engineering.** Until 9,000 N became
  21 MPa and "quick" became 10–15 s, the three material classes could not
  even be compared.
- **Match the physics to the load scale.** Nanonewton molecular switches and
  a 9 kN impact live in different worlds; structural colour bridges the gap.
- **Elimination is a result.** Two of three classes fell to hard,
  quantitative mismatches — that is the study working, not failing.
- **Know where the desk stops.** The final ranking is honest about needing
  experiments; overselling a literature result helps no one.
